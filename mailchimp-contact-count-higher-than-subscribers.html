Why your Mailchimp contact count is higher than your subscriber count

Run the free audit — browser-only, nothing uploaded

Your Mailchimp plan is priced on contacts, not subscribers. Mailchimp defines your contact total as the number of contacts stored across all of your audiences, and subscribed, unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts are all included in your contact count (About Mailchimp Pricing Plans). So the number you pay for is usually bigger than the number of people you can email.

What fills the gap

  • Unsubscribed contacts. People who received your marketing and then opted out. They can't receive marketing emails, but they count toward your monthly contact limit (About Your Contacts).
  • Non-subscribed contacts. People who interacted with you but never opted in, for example through your online store, an integration, a manual import, or a forwarded email to your Mailchimp Inbox. They count toward the limit too, and they have no badge in the Email Marketing or SMS Marketing columns of the contact table (source).
  • More than one audience. The contact total adds up every audience (source), and Mailchimp doesn't scan for duplicates across audiences: the same email in two audiences is two people (About Duplicate Contacts).

What doesn't count: cleaned, pending, archived and deleted contacts (About Your Contacts). Pending contacts aren't added to your audience until they confirm, and they're deleted after 60 days if they don't (same source). Legacy paid plans created before May 15, 2019 may follow different contact-limit rules (same source).

See the breakdown in Mailchimp

  1. Click Audience, and pick the audience from the Audience drop-down if you have more than one.
  2. Find the Email Marketing column in the contact table and click it to sort the audience by contact type. The badge shows each contact's marketing status (source).
  3. For non-subscribed contacts, which have no badge, Mailchimp's own method is to build a segment of them, which you can save or export as a CSV (About Non-Subscribed Contacts).
  4. Repeat for each audience. Your overall position against your plan is on the Account Overview page, which shows how close you are to your contact limits (About Mailchimp Pricing Tiers).

Bring the count down

  • Archived contacts don't count toward your contact total or the price of your plan (source). You can unarchive them later, and a contact in more than one audience has to be archived from each audience (Archive or Unarchive Your Contacts).
  • If you only use Mailchimp for bulk email, Mailchimp says you may get more value from archiving your segment of non-subscribed contacts, which might let you move to a lower pricing tier (source).
  • Step-by-step: archive unsubscribed contacts in bulk, find duplicates across audiences.

FAQ

Does Mailchimp charge for subscribers or for contacts? For contacts. Subscribed, unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts across all your audiences make up the contact total your plan is priced on.

Why do some Mailchimp contacts have no badge in the Email Marketing column? Those are non-subscribed contacts: people who interacted with you but never opted in to marketing. They still count toward your contact limit.

Do pending or cleaned contacts count toward my Mailchimp contact limit? No. Cleaned, pending, archived and deleted contacts don't count.

Does the same email in two Mailchimp audiences count twice? Yes. Mailchimp doesn't check for duplicates across audiences, so the same email in two audiences is two contacts.
